Business email and domain: how to set them up properly

An address like name@company is the first thing a client sees after your website. But the DNS records behind it matter more than the address: without them even legitimate email lands in spam. Here is how to set up business email and a domain.

Sikra · Updated 2026-09-18

Choosing a domain

  • Short and easy to spell out over the phone, without hyphens or digits.
  • On a TLD your clients trust: .com, your country code or an industry TLD.
  • Registered to the company, with auto-renewal switched on.
  • DNS kept with a reliable provider such as Cloudflare.

Choosing an email provider

The most common options are Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Zoho Mail. They differ in interface, office apps and price per user. For a small team the brand matters less than having every mailbox, alias such as info@ and sales@, and shared calendar in one place.

DNS records: MX, SPF, DKIM, DMARC

Since 2024 Gmail and Yahoo require SPF, DKIM and DMARC from bulk senders. Even if you only email clients by hand, deliverability is noticeably worse without them.

  • MX — tells the world which server receives mail for the domain.
  • SPF — lists the servers allowed to send mail on behalf of the domain.
  • DKIM — a cryptographic signature proving the message was not tampered with.
  • DMARC — the policy for messages that fail those checks, plus reports on spoofing attempts.

Campaigns and transactional email

Your campaign tool and website notifications (confirmations, receipts) must also be included in SPF and signed with DKIM. Bulk campaigns are often sent from a separate subdomain so that campaign issues do not hurt day-to-day email.

Frequently asked questions

Why does email from my company domain go to spam?

Most often SPF, DKIM and DMARC are missing or misconfigured, or the domain is new and has no reputation yet. Checking and fixing the DNS records usually solves it.

Can I move email to another provider without losing messages?

Yes. Messages are copied with migration tools and the MX records are switched last, so no new mail is lost.

How many mailboxes does a small company need?

One personal mailbox per employee, plus free aliases such as info@, sales@ and support@ forwarded to the right people.
